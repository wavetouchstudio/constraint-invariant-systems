Abstract

“The Integrity Tax”

Where the previous paper (IV.03) traced how institutions convert help-seeking into liability, this paper turns to voluntary, informal relationships and shows the same extractive topology forming without any institution at all—here, competence itself becomes the liability. It presents a structural model explaining how extractive, cult-like dependency can form around an intelligent, honest, ethically responsive individual ("High-Signal Agent," HSA) without the HSA engaging in manipulation, ideology, or malicious intent. The mechanism is driven by six primitives: (1) the HSA's role as a high-quality compression node for unresolved complexity; (2) a commons-style aggregate load problem where each request is locally reasonable but the total burden is unsustainable; (3) moral capture where ethics becomes leverage; (4) the transition from access-as-gift to access-as-entitlement; (5) third-party narration and proxy authority; and (6) withdrawal hostility as a system defence. The model is expanded with why HSAs remain engaged beyond early warning signs (incentive structure, not blame); a clear delineation of healthy asymmetry versus extraction; a decision-oriented prevention–treatment framework; a refined legal-criminal exposure analysis that foregrounds communications as evidence, timing windows, and the "good Samaritan becomes facilitator" reframing; and a new online-HSA model where algorithmic attention, parasocial entitlement, and adversarial swarms produce coordinated smear dynamics and accelerate destabilisation.

1. Scope, Assumptions, and Non-Attribution

The paper does not require malicious intent by any actor, ideological, doctrinal, or belief enforcement, charismatic domination or leadership claims, or psychopathology or mass delusion accounts. Participants are treated as bounded agents who respond to incentives, norms, and informational scarcity. "Cult" is used functionally (dependency topology), not culturally.

2. Definitions and Primitives

2.1 High-Signal Agent (HSA)

An agent whose outputs exhibit high coherence, low contradiction, and reliable good-faith engagement, providing stable interpretive compression across contexts.

2.2 Compression Node

A social locus where unresolved complexity is offloaded cheaply. HSAs become compression nodes when they reliably produce high-quality reductions of confusion into actionable models.

2.3 Asymmetric Extraction

A one-directional flow of cognitive labour, emotional regulation, legitimacy, and interpretive authority, without an explicit scope, compensation, or reciprocity.

2.4 Moral Capture

A mechanism whereby ethical responsiveness is converted into obligation through harm-prevention framing and responsibility gradients.

2.5 Cult (Functional Definition)

A structure characterised by centralised dependence on a focal agent, moralised access, hostility to withdrawal, and externalisation of responsibility onto the focal agent, independent of belief content.

3. Initial Conditions: Signal-Density Meets Cognitive Scarcity

The ecological mismatch is:

ElementObservation
HSA compresses and clarifies effectively✔︎
Weak norms for integration and epistemic responsibility✔︎
Few substitute compression nodes✔︎
Social reward for asking, not learning✔︎

This commons problem yields a locally reasonable request but a globally destructive aggregate load.

4. Phase I – Passive Aggregation

The early state appears healthy: increased questions, appreciation, informal deference, and voluntary explanations. Interpretive gravity begins to form, and the HSA becomes the default stabiliser.

5. Phase II – Reciprocity Failure and Lock-In

The transition from healthy asymmetry to extraction is driven by predictors in three layers.

5.1 Context Predictors

Group size and growth diffuse responsibility; scarcity of substitutes leaves no alternative mentors; local norms reward asking over integrating; incentives reward status from proximity rather than mastery; and the cost of repeated misunderstanding stays low.

5.2 HSA Predictors

High availability and rapid response cadence, discomfort with letting errors persist, identity investment in "being helpful," low tolerance for unresolved misinterpretation, and weak boundary formalisation all pull in the same direction.

5.3 Participant Predictors

Cognitive capacity variance, low metacognitive awareness, conflict avoidance via proxy authority ("X would say…"), and social learning norms that reward deference over competence complete the picture. Lock-in occurs when repeated extraction is not penalised by friction.

6. HSA's Persistence Instructions (Nuanced Contribution, No Blame)

HSAs remain engaged because of internal incentives, not pathology: an optimization instinct ("better explanation will fix it"), a responsibility gradient ("if I don't clarify, harm follows"), a pattern-completion drive ("contradiction must be resolvable"), a curiosity trap where "why don't they understand?" becomes its own puzzle, and identity reinforcement where "explainer" becomes self-concept.

7. Access → Expectation → Entitlement (Endowment Transition)

  1. Gift. Access feels generous.

  2. Pattern. Repetition normalises availability.

  3. Expectation. Non-response feels like deprivation.

  4. Entitlement. Expectation turns into a moral claim.

The endowment effect on cognitive labour means psychological ownership of reliable access causes perceived harm when removed.

8. Third-Party Narration – Proxy Authority Emergence

Proxy authority ("X thinks…", "X would say…") arises because of status and proximity signalling, cognitive offloading that avoids reasoning, conflict weaponisation as a cheap dispute resolution, boundary ambiguity where silence reads as endorsement, and the sheer convenience it offers for group coordination. This centralises risk and liability on the HSA.

9. Moral Capture Loop

The loop runs from confusion to distress to HSA intervention to reinforced dependence. Ethical responsiveness becomes a predictable control surface; misunderstanding functions as a summoning ritual.

10. Clarification as Accelerator (Good-Faith Accelerator)

Clarification increases extraction by lowering the cost of not integrating, rewarding repeated misunderstanding, heightening perceived supervision, and deepening the HSA's centrality in the network. Conscientious people become trapped because they inject more content to solve a topological problem.

11. Withdrawal Hostility – System Defense

When an HSA disengages, the system responds with betrayal narratives, guilt induction, moral accusation, and escalated misrepresentation—a defence to the removal of a load-bearing substrate.

12. Recovery and Repair (Post-Disengagement)

12.1 HSA Experience

Guilt at perceived abandonment gives way to relief as the air returns, disorientation at why it was so hard, and pattern recognition once the dynamic becomes visible everywhere.

12.2 Dependent System Experience

Hostility moves toward bargaining, narrative rewriting ("you were never helpful"), and substitution attempts (finding a new HSA), with rare growth via distributed capacity building.

12.3 Healthy Resolution Conditions

Resolution depends on an explicit norm reset, the existence of substitutes, time passing without re-engagement, and responsibility being redistributed and enforced.

13. Prevention vs Treatment (Pragmatic Immunology)

13.1 Prevention (Early)

Bounded scope ("I'll explain once; afterward use resources"), response latency that avoids signaling instant availability, refusal to adjudicate disputes ("don't quote me to win arguments"), strategic non-correction that lets misunderstanding persist, and redirection to other experts and written resources.

13.2 Treatment (Late)

Once extraction is established, the remaining tools are hard boundaries (often escalatory), strategic withdrawal, formalisation of role, scope, compensation, and delegation where appropriate, and system redesign toward multiple compression nodes.

14. Healthy Asymmetry vs Extraction

A sustainable HSA presence requires five stabilisers:

  1. Explicit scope (topic boundaries, time bounds).

  2. Integration requirements that demonstrate learning.

  3. Cost signals (friction, finite access).

  4. Distributed authority (multiple experts, no single point of failure).

  5. Reciprocity (compensation, peer contribution, or status balancing).

Absent any of these, informal expertise relationships drift toward extraction.

15. Institutional Parallels – Structural Exploitation Without Exploiters

Organizations replicate the topology via "go-to-person" dynamics: beneficiaries distributed, costs concentrated, knowledge systems poor, culture rewarding helpfulness, and boundary-setting punished. The system behaves as if it exploits the HSA, even when no one intends it.

16. Legal and Criminal Exposure (Conceptual, Not Advice)

16.1 Moral Authority → Legal Liability

Courts infer authority from centrality in communications, perceived supervision, expertise, and sustained association. An HSA's competence defeats ignorance narratives; helpfulness creates records.

16.2 Communications as Evidence

Every clarification generates timestamped evidence of knowledge, detail awareness, ongoing involvement, and perceived influence. Benign harm-reduction can be reinterpreted as operational enablement.

16.3 Timing Window

Exposure rises across the relationship's life: early involvement carries low exposure, mid-stage involvement rising exposure as knowledge records and centrality accumulate, late involvement high exposure through attribution and association, and once a crisis hits, every move is scrutinised. The window closes faster than HSAs expect because they delay exit.

16.4 "Good Samaritan → Knowing Facilitator" Reframing

Under scrutiny, "explaining risk" is reframed as "advising risk mitigation," "preventing harm" as "making the operation safer," and "trying to help exit" as "maintaining involvement." Intent is less important than legibility.

16.5 Documentation Paradox

Documenting objections can prove non-approval but also prove knowledge. It is context-specific; there is no universal safe rule.

16.6 Decision Signal Set

If any of the following are present, the situation moves from social to legal:

  • Discussion of illegal conduct.

  • Story alignment requests.

  • Deletion of communications.

  • Contact by investigators or lawyers.

  • "You know too much" pressure.

  • Threats of implication if you leave.

17. Online HSA Dynamics

17.1 Scaling Law – Broadcast Node

Offline extraction is limited by proximity. Online, asymmetrical audience size, persistent content, searchable receipts, and cross-platform replication amplify extraction to a virtually infinite request volume.

17.2 Parasocial Entitlement

Online audiences feel personally addressed without reciprocal bonding, accelerating the endowment transition with little friction or empathy.

17.3 Adversarial Recruitment Effect

Visibility attracts actors who optimise for status, conflict, or chaos. Algorithms coordinate by selecting emotionally activating content, creating a mixing chamber that recruits opportunists, grifters, dogpilers, enforcers, and harassment clusters.

17.4 Smear Campaign Formation (Without Planner)

Negative content spreads faster; moral outrage binds groups; ambiguity allows projection; simplified villain narratives outcompete nuance. Swarm behaviour produces clip-mining, quote-tweet tribunals, guilt-by-association graphs, and manufactured pattern narratives.

17.5 Centrality as Attack Surface

Competence defeats plausible deniability. Consistency enables selective framing ("they always say…"). Ethical engagement generates receipts; correcting misinformation increases exposure.

17.6 Reply Trap

Public clarification feeds platform engagement algorithms, provides evidence for opponents, invites more demands, and signals availability—the online version of "clarification makes it worse."

17.7 Online Withdrawal Hostility

Silence is interpreted as betrayal, guilt, or admission, imposing an illegitimate "public duty of response" on the HSA.

17.8 Risk Escalation – Doxxing, Stalking, Legal Targeting

Swarm dynamics heighten doxxing, stalking, false reporting, and administrative attacks. Public records become legal or employer pressure tools, turning social extraction into reputational, professional, and legal destruction.

17.9 Defensive Design Principles for Online HSAs

  • Asynchronous, slow cadence.

  • Public scope and boundaries.

  • Refusal to adjudicate disputes.

  • Avoid reactive engagement loops.

  • Distributed moderators or co-authors.

  • Separate personal identity from content identity.

  • Minimise private DMs ("confessional" channels).

  • Treat any written content as potentially public evidence.

The goal: prevent becoming a single-point-of-failure target.

18. Synthesis – Structure Without Villains, With Adversaries

Offline, this is structural exploitation without exploiters. Online, the same structure reliably introduces true adversaries because attention economies reward them. Algorithms and incentives produce coordination-like outcomes, even when no explicit coordination exists.

Concluding Analysis

Extraction of HSAs is not a pathology of leaders or followers but a topology that emerges when compression quality is rare, integration norms are weak, access is frictionless, ethics is leveraged, and centrality becomes legible in communications. When crime-adjacent behaviour exists, moral centrality can become legal liability, and when online presence exists, algorithmic amplification recruits adversarial swarms and turns clarification into fuel. Stable solutions—explicit scope, cost signals, distributed authority, and boundary design—rather than better explanation alone, are required for a sustainable, non-extractive HSA role.

Direct Answers to the Two Biggest "Paper Questions"

1. Is there a sustainable, non-extractive HSA role?

Yes, only with explicit scope, cost signals, and distributed authority. Without those, centralisation into extraction is the default.

2. Does every informal expertise relationship eventually extract?

Not inevitably, but the risk rises sharply with rare compression quality, frictionless access, and weak integration norms. "Informal" is not the problem; "unbounded" is.

Conclusion

“The Integrity Tax”

When a single coherent individual offers frictionless, unbounded access to rare, high-quality knowledge in an environment with weak integration norms, the informal expert-learner topology naturally drifts toward extraction—followers rely on the expert for ongoing clarification while the expert bears the cost of continual learning. The contribution of High-Signal Informal Expertise and Asymmetric Extraction is therefore a structured account of its governing constraints, mechanisms, and failure conditions. Its claims remain bounded by the evidence and counterexamples specified in the paper, and its practical value depends on whether the proposed relationships improve explanation, prediction, or correction in the relevant domain. The axis now turns from this public, voluntary register to its most private and highest-stakes form: the next paper (IV.05) examines how the same disclosure-suppressing structure appears inside a family of origin.
