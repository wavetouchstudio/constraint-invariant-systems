# Abstract

“The Integrity Tax”

This paper presents a structural model explaining how extractive,
cult‑like dependency can form around an intelligent, honest, ethically
responsive individual (“High‑Signal Agent,” HSA) without the HSA
engaging in manipulation, ideology, or malicious intent. The mechanism
is driven by six primitives: (1) the HSA’s role as a high‑quality
compression node for unresolved complexity; (2) a commons‑style
aggregate load problem where each request is locally reasonable but the
total burden is unsustainable; (3) moral capture where ethics becomes
leverage; (4) the transition from access‑as‑gift to
access‑as‑entitlement; (5) third‑party narration and proxy authority;
and (6) withdrawal hostility as a system defence. The model is expanded
with: (a) why HSAs remain engaged beyond early warning signs (incentive
structure, not blame); (b) a clear delineation of healthy asymmetry
versus extraction; (c) a decision‑oriented prevention–treatment
framework; (d) a refined legal‑criminal exposure analysis that
foregrounds communications as evidence, timing windows, and the “good
Samaritan becomes facilitator” reframing; and (e) a new online‑HSA model
where algorithmic attention, parasocial entitlement, and adversarial
swarms produce coordinated smear dynamics and accelerate
destabilisation.

# 1. SCOPE, ASSUMPTIONS, AND NON‑ATTRIBUTION

“The Integrity Tax”

The paper does not require any of the following:

Malicious intent by any actor

Ideological, doctrinal, or belief enforcement

Charismatic domination or leadership claims

Psychopathology or mass delusion accounts

Participants are treated as bounded agents who respond to incentives,
norms, and informational scarcity. “Cult” is used functionally
(dependency topology), not culturally.

# 2. DEFINITIONS AND PRIMITIVES

“The Integrity Tax”

## 2.1 HIGH‑SIGNAL AGENT (HSA)

An agent whose outputs exhibit high coherence, low contradiction, and
reliable good‑faith engagement, providing stable interpretive
compression across contexts.

## 2.2 COMPRESSION NODE

A social locus where unresolved complexity is offloaded cheaply. HSAs
become compression nodes when they reliably produce high‑quality
reductions of confusion into actionable models.

## 2.3 ASYMMETRIC EXTRACTION

A one‑directional flow of:

Cognitive labour

Emotional regulation

Legitimacy

Interpretive authority

without an explicit scope, compensation, or reciprocity.

## 2.4 MORAL CAPTURE

A mechanism whereby ethical responsiveness is converted into obligation
through harm‑prevention framing and responsibility gradients.

## 2.5 CULT (FUNCTIONAL DEFINITION)

A structure characterised by:

Centralised dependence on a focal agent

Moralised access

Hostility to withdrawal

Externalisation of responsibility onto the focal agent

independent of belief content.

# 5. PHASE II – RECIPROCITY FAILURE AND LOCK‑IN

“The Integrity Tax”

The transition from healthy asymmetry to extraction is driven by
predictors in three layers.

## 5.1 Context Predictors

Group size and growth (diffusion of responsibility)

Scarcity of substitutes (no alternative mentors)

Norms: asking \> integrating

Incentives: status from proximity, not mastery

Low cost to repeated misunderstanding

## 5.2 HSA Predictors

High availability / rapid response cadence

Discomfort with letting errors persist

Identity investment in “being helpful”

Low tolerance for unresolved misinterpretation

Weak boundary formalisation

## 5.3 Participant Predictors

Cognitive capacity variance

Low metacognitive awareness

Conflict avoidance via proxy authority (“X would say…”)

Social learning norms rewarding deference over competence

Lock‑in occurs when repeated extraction is not penalised by friction.

# 6. HSA’S PERSISTENCE INSTRUCTIONS (NUANCED CONTRIBUTION, NO BLAME)

# 13. PREVENTION VS TREATMENT (PRAGMATIC IMMUNOLOGY)

“The Integrity Tax”

Response latency (no instant availability signal)

Refusal to adjudicate disputes (“don’t quote me to win arguments”)

Strategic non‑correction (let misunderstanding persist)

Strategic withdrawal

Formalisation (role, scope, compensation, delegation) where appropriate

System redesign (multiple compression nodes)

# 16. LEGAL AND CRIMINAL EXPOSURE (CONCEPTUAL, NOT ADVICE)

“The Integrity Tax”

## 16.1 Moral Authority → Legal Liability

Courts infer authority from: centrality in communications, perceived
supervision, expertise, and sustained association. An HSA’s competence
defeats ignorance narratives; helpfulness creates records.

## 16.2 Communications as Evidence

Every clarification generates timestamped evidence of: knowledge, detail
awareness, ongoing involvement, and perceived influence. Benign
harm‑reduction can be reinterpreted as operational enablement.

## 16.3 Timing Window

Early: low exposure

Mid: rising exposure (knowledge records & centrality)

Late: high exposure (attribution, association)

Crisis: every move scrutinised

The window closes faster than HSAs expect because they delay exit.

16.4 “Good Samaritan → Knowing Facilitator” Reframing

“Explaining risk” → “Advising risk mitigation”

“Preventing harm” → “Making the operation safer”

“Trying to help exit” → “Maintaining involvement”

Intent is less important than legibility.

## 16.5 Documentation Paradox

Documenting objections can prove non‑approval but also prove knowledge.
Context‑specific; no universal safe rule.

## 16.6 Decision Signal Set

If any of the following are present, the situation moves from social to
legal:

Discussion of illegal conduct

Story alignment requests

Deletion of communications

Contact by investigators or lawyers

“You know too much” pressure

Threats of implication if you leave

# 17. ONLINE HSA DYNAMICS

“The Integrity Tax”

## 17.1 Scaling Law – Broadcast Node

Offline extraction limited by proximity. Online, asymmetrical audience
size, persistent content, searchable receipts, and cross‑platform
replication amplify extraction to a virtually infinite request volume.

## 17.2 Parasocial Entitlement

Online audiences feel personally addressed without reciprocal bonding,
accelerating the endowment transition with little friction or empathy.

## 17.3 Adversarial Recruitment Effect

Visibility attracts actors who optimise for status, conflict, or chaos.
Algorithms coordinate by selecting emotionally activating content,
creating a mixing chamber that recruits opportunists, grifters,
dogpilers, enforcers, and harassment clusters.

## 17.4 Smear Campaign Formation (Without Planner)

Negative content spreads faster; moral outrage binds groups; ambiguity
allows projection; simplified villain narratives outcompete nuance.
Swarm behaviour produces clip‑mining, quote‑tweet tribunals,
guilt‑by‑association graphs, and manufactured pattern narratives.

## 17.5 Centrality as Attack Surface

Competence defeats plausible deniability. Consistency enables selective
framing (“they always say…”). Ethical engagement generates receipts;
correcting misinformation increases exposure.

## 17.6 Reply Trap

Public clarification feeds platform engagement algorithms, provides
evidence for opponents, invites more demands, and signals availability –
the online version of “clarification makes it worse.”

## 17.7 Online Withdrawal Hostility

Silence is interpreted as betrayal, guilt, or admission, imposing an
illegitimate “public duty of response” on the HSA.

## 17.8 Risk Escalation – Doxxing, Stalking, Legal Targeting

Swarm dynamics heighten doxxing, stalking, false reporting, and
administrative attacks. Public records become legal or employer pressure
tools, turning social extraction into reputational, professional, and
legal destruction.

## 17.9 Defensive Design Principles for Online HSAs

Asynchronous, slow cadence

Public scope and boundaries

Refusal to adjudicate disputes

Avoid reactive engagement loops

Distributed moderators or co‑authors

Separate personal identity from content identity

Minimise private DMs (“confessional” channels)

Treat any written content as potentially public evidence

The goal: prevent becoming a single‑point‑of‑failure target.

# 18. SYNTHESIS – STRUCTURE WITHOUT VILLAINS, WITH ADVERSARIES

“The Integrity Tax”

Offline: structural exploitation without exploiters.

Online: the same structure reliably introduces true adversaries because
attention economies reward them. Algorithms and incentives produce
coordination‑like outcomes, even when no explicit coordination exists.

## Concluding Analysis

Extraction of HSAs is not a pathology of leaders or followers but a
topology that emerges when:

Compression quality is rare

Integration norms are weak

Access is frictionless

Ethics is leveraged

Centrality becomes legible in communications

When crime‑adjacent behaviour exists, moral centrality can become legal
liability.

When online presence exists, algorithmic amplification recruits
adversarial swarms and turns clarification into fuel.

Stable solutions: explicit scope, cost signals, distributed authority,
and boundary design—rather than better explanation alone—are required
for a sustainable, non‑extractive HSA role.

# DIRECT ANSWERS TO THE TWO BIGGEST “PAPER QUESTIONS”

“The Integrity Tax”

# 1. Is there a sustainable, non‑extractive HSA role?

“The Integrity Tax”

Yes, only with explicit scope, cost signals, and distributed authority.
Without those, centralisation → extraction is the default.

# 2. Does every informal expertise relationship eventually extract?

“The Integrity Tax”

Not inevitably, but the risk rises sharply with rare compression
quality, frictionless access, and weak integration norms. “Informal” is
not the problem; “unbounded” is.

# Conclusion

“The Integrity Tax”

When a single coherent individual offers frictionless, unbounded access
to rare, high-quality knowledge in an environment with weak integration
norms, the informal expert-learner topology naturally drifts toward
extraction—followers rely on the expert for ongoing clarification while
the expert bears the cost of continual learning. The contribution of
High-Signal Informal Expertise and Asymmetric Extraction is therefore a
structured account of its governing constraints, mechanisms, and failure
conditions. Its claims remain bounded by the evidence and
counterexamples specified in the paper, and its practical value depends
on whether the proposed relationships improve explanation, prediction,
or correction in the relevant domain.
