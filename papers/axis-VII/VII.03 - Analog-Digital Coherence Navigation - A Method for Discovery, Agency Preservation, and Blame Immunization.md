VII.03 - Analog–Digital Coherence Navigation: A Method for Discovery,
Agency Preservation, and Blame Immunization

A cyclic protocol for pre-formal search, constraint testing, residual
analysis, and responsibility localization

# Abstract

“Crossing the Seam Without Tearing Reality”

Following the structural account of agency under noise and symbolic
capture in the previous paper of this axis (VII.02), this paper turns to
method: how an agent or an inquiry can search open terrain without
either freezing prematurely or drifting into ungrounded narrative. This
paper presents Analog-Digital Coherence Navigation, a proposed method
for discovery in complex or underdetermined problem spaces. The method
alternates analog expansion, which generates low-commitment candidate
structures from weak signals, metaphor, and semantic association, with
digital constraint, which tests candidates against definitions, logic,
evidence, and traceable responsibility. Residual analysis preserves what
remains unresolved after constraint as input to the next cycle instead
of forcing premature closure. A governing attribution rule - formal
enablement does not by itself establish event-level authorship - is used
to keep contextual explanation from dissolving local responsibility. The
paper also operationalizes a Narrative Displacement Index as a proposed
diagnostic for explanations that rely on abstract actors while omitting
identifiable decisions. Applications to organizational, family,
workplace, technical, and metaphysical inquiry illustrate the protocol
without validating it. The framework is a methodological proposal and
hypothesis generator, not a demonstrated discovery advantage, a general
theory of causation, or a mechanism for immunizing agents from warranted
accountability.

## **1. Discovery Under Premature Closure**

Difficult inquiries often begin before the relevant variables,
categories, or measurement procedures are stable. A method that demands
complete formalization at the outset can exclude novel structures
because it only searches what has already been parameterized. A method
that relies only on association can produce the opposite failure:
apparent coherence accumulates faster than disconfirming pressure. The
source identifies four recurring breakdowns:

1.  Premature formalism filters out candidates that cannot yet be
    expressed in the available variables or rules.

2.  Unconstrained intuition cannot reliably distinguish a promising
    pattern from noise, projection, or narrative closure.

3.  Hybrid discovery remains implicit, so teams present an exploratory
    path as if it had been deductive from the beginning.

4.  Responsibility is displaced into abstractions when a structure's
    enabling role is treated as sufficient proof that it authored a
    particular act.

Analog-Digital Coherence Navigation makes the hybrid explicit. It
separates candidate generation from candidate testing while requiring
each cycle to preserve an account of who or what supplied constraints,
who made consequential selections, what evidence pruned the model, and
what remains unresolved.

### **1.1 Contribution and epistemic status**

The contribution is procedural: a cyclic search architecture, an
attribution discipline, a residual log, and proposed diagnostics for
evaluating convergence and responsibility localization. The source
contains no comparative studies, validation data, citations, or
thresholds demonstrating that this method outperforms alternatives.

**Epistemic boundary.** The protocol is a model for organizing inquiry.
Its mechanisms are proposals, its evaluation criteria are provisional,
its examples are illustrative, and its predictions require operational
definitions and comparison conditions.

## **2. Definitions and Attribution Roles**

**Analog exploration.** A pre-formal reasoning mode that keeps multiple
candidate meanings or structures available long enough to detect
possible relations. It is continuous, multivalent, directional, and
tolerant of ambiguity, but it does not grant truth status to the
candidates it generates.

**Digital constraint.** A testing mode that converts a candidate into
sufficiently explicit claims, definitions, exclusions, and
responsibility assignments to permit logical, empirical, and procedural
challenge.

**Residual.** An unresolved structure, conflict, exception, or
unexplained remainder that survives a constraint pass. A residual is a
search signal, not evidence that the candidate is true or that every
discarded element should be restored.

**Navigational coherence.** Sufficient compatibility among definitions,
evidence, constraints, and responsibility assignments for a model to
guide the next inquiry or decision. It does not by itself establish
empirical truth, moral goodness, consensus, or metaphysical alignment.

**Constraint context.** The physical limits, rules, incentives,
information conditions, affordances, and learned pressures that shape
which actions are available, visible, costly, or likely.

**Formal enablement.** The inclusion of an action within a system's
possibility space. Enablement can be causally relevant in a broad
explanatory sense, but permission or feasibility alone does not
establish who selected, authorized, executed, or knowingly maintained a
particular outcome.

**Local or event authorship.** A traceable contribution to a
consequential selection, authorization, execution, concealment,
supervision, or omission at a particular decision point. Authorship can
be distributed across multiple agents and can coexist with material
constraint, coercion, or diminished capacity.

**Blame immunization.** The source's name for resistance to recursive or
mislocalized blame. In this paper it is a property of an attribution
process, not immunity for a person, institution, designer, leader, or
system from supported causal or normative responsibility.

Table 1. Analog and digital reasoning roles

| **Dimension** | **Analog exploration** | **Digital constraint** |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Primary task | Generate and connect candidate structures. | Make candidates testable and remove failures. |
| Semantic posture | Multiple meanings may remain active provisionally. | Terms are narrowed enough for explicit evaluation. |
| Evidence posture | Weak signals may guide attention without warranting belief. | Claims face consistency, evidence, and alternative-explanation checks. |
| Characteristic risk | Projection, narrative substitution, and self-sealing coherence. | Premature closure, false precision, and exclusion of unparameterized novelty. |
| Required handoff | Candidate map with uncertainty retained. | Decision record identifying rejection, retention, and residuals. |

### **2.1 The governing attribution rule**

The source states the invariant as 'enablement is not causation' while
defining causation as authorship or efficient cause. That wording is too
broad if read as a general causal claim: policies, incentives,
interfaces, algorithms, and material conditions can contribute causally
to outcomes even when they do not literally choose. The operational
invariant used here is narrower and more defensible:

**Attribution invariant.** Formal enablement alone is insufficient to
establish event-level authorship. A complete account should identify
both the constraint context and any traceable selections,
authorizations, executions, maintenance decisions, or omissions.

This distinction prevents two symmetrical errors. Pure structural
attribution can erase identifiable decisions; pure individual
attribution can erase coercion, option restriction, predictable
incentives, and responsibilities attached to designing or maintaining
the context. The protocol therefore localizes responsibility without
pretending that context is irrelevant.

## **3. The Analog-Digital Cycle**

The method is iterative rather than a one-way funnel. Each pass
generates, tests, records, and reopens candidates through four phases:

1.  Analog expansion. Generate candidate structures and relationships
    without prematurely demanding a complete formal account.

2.  Digital constraint. Test candidates against non-negotiable facts,
    definitions, logic, counterexamples, and responsibility
    localization.

3.  Residual analysis. Record the unresolved remainder, including
    exceptions, missing variables, incompatible constraints, and
    promising failures.

4.  Iteration. Use the residual to revise the search surface, then
    repeat until the model is stable enough for its stated use or
    inquiry resources are exhausted.

Table 2. Outputs and guardrails for each protocol phase

| **Phase** | **Primary output** | **Guardrail** | **Reset condition** |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Analog expansion | Low-commitment candidate map | Separate attention-guiding associations from claims. | A symbol or metaphor is treated as proof. |
| Digital constraint | Explicit accept, reject, or revise decisions | Test alternatives and preserve causal-role distinctions. | Existing parameters exclude novelty by definition. |
| Residual analysis | Prioritized unresolved questions | Retain only remainders traceable to a failed or incomplete test. | Every anomaly is promoted to high-value signal. |
| Iteration | Revised model and decision record | State the use criterion and stopping rule. | Coherence is declared without external challenge. |

### **3.1 Why the cycle may improve inquiry**

The protocol assigns search and stabilization to different operations.
Analog exploration can surface relations that a fixed variable set would
miss. Digital constraint can prevent those relations from hardening into
protected stories. Residual analysis preserves the information carried
by a failed test: the model may lack a variable, combine different
causal levels, use an unstable definition, or face a genuine exception.

This is a plausible design rationale, not validation. The cycle can also
waste effort, amplify motivated pattern detection, or repeatedly reopen
questions that should remain closed. Its performance depends on the
quality and independence of constraints, the cost of testing, the
competence of participants, and whether stopping criteria are specified
before a preferred conclusion appears.

### **3.2 Stability and stopping**

**Directional closure.** Enough navigational coherence to support the
next bounded decision while keeping material residuals visible.
Directional closure is not a claim to have exhausted reality.

A cycle can stop provisionally when the model satisfies its declared
use, survives the available constraint tests, retains explicit
uncertainty, and assigns consequential decisions to traceable roles.
Stability should be reopened when new evidence, a failed prediction, an
unrepresented stakeholder, or a previously hidden decision point changes
those conditions.

## **4. Recursive Blame and Dual Attribution**

### **4.1 The recursive category error**

The source describes a scale-repeating attribution failure. Restated
with the narrower invariant, the pattern is:

1.  A structure S shapes a possibility space P through rules, resources,
    incentives, or limits.

2.  One or more agents select, authorize, execute, maintain, or omit
    action a within P.

3.  The explanation attributes authorship only to S, without identifying
    the relevant decision points.

4.  Responsibility migrates to a more abstract structure S-prime, while
    the local and design-level contributions remain unspecified.

5.  The migration repeats until accountability becomes either totalized
    at the highest abstraction or dissolved entirely.

The likely consequence is not necessarily 'cosmic blame,' nihilism, or
violence in every case. The defensible claim is conditional: repeated
abstraction can make correction difficult because no accountable role,
decision, or appeal path remains. It can also support scapegoating when
a complex outcome is assigned to one symbolic target while other
contributors disappear.

### **4.2 Responsibility localization rule**

For a consequential event, the protocol requires two linked accounts:

- Constraint context: what rules, incentives, resource limits,
  information asymmetries, dependencies, or coercive pressures shaped
  the available actions?

- Authorship map: who selected, authorized, executed, maintained,
  concealed, supervised, or could reasonably have corrected the relevant
  act?

Dual attribution is not a formula for equal blame. It separates roles so
that evidence can support different causal, legal, organizational, or
ethical judgments. A person may retain event-level authorship while
having sharply constrained options. A leader may not execute an act but
may author policy, incentives, authorization, negligent oversight, or
continued maintenance. A system label is useful only when it can be
decomposed into mechanisms and responsible roles.

Table 3. Attribution roles and common collapses

| **Role** | **Question** | **Relevant evidence** | **Collapse to avoid** |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Constraint context | What shaped feasibility and cost? | Rules, incentives, access, information, coercion, dependencies | Treating context as irrelevant or as a choosing person |
| Selection | Who chose among available options? | Decision records, instructions, admissions, observed acts | Inferring choice from mere presence in a system |
| Authorization | Who permitted or required the act? | Delegations, approvals, mandates, escalation records | Equating permission with execution or denying authorial contribution |
| Execution | Who carried out the act? | Actions, logs, transactions, direct observation | Treating execution as the only responsibility-bearing role |
| Maintenance or omission | Who knowingly sustained or failed to correct the condition? | Notice, authority, capacity, duty, and response history | Assigning omission without evidence of knowledge or capacity |

## **5. Language as a Controlled Search Field**

### **5.1 Semantic superposition**

During analog exploration, a word can provisionally carry several
related senses. Metaphor, etymology, sound, contrast, and symbolic
association may reveal candidate structural parallels before the terms
are narrowed. The technique is useful only as hypothesis-space
navigation. It does not imply that linguistic resemblance reveals a
causal connection, historical relation, hidden ontology, or shared
mechanism.

**Operational rule.** Symbols guide attention, not belief. Every
association that survives analog exploration must later be restated as a
claim whose terms, evidence requirements, alternatives, and scope can be
challenged.

### **5.2 Constraint on semantic exploration**

A linguistic move can remain in the working model only if it survives
the attribution invariant, internal consistency, relevant evidence, and
robustness outside the example that produced it. Cross-domain similarity
is not enough: the analyst must state which relation is shared, which
features differ, and what observation would make the analogy unhelpful.

- Tag metaphor, etymology, resonance, and analogy as search heuristics.

- Translate a promising association into explicit variables or relations
  before using it to support a decision.

- Check whether the same candidate appears under different language and
  whether the language creates the apparent pattern.

- Record discarded symbolic links so they do not quietly return as
  premises in later cycles.

## **6. Intuition as Pre-Formal Gradient Detection**

**Intuition (proposed operationalization).** The capacity to notice a
possible increase in model coherence before the relevant relation can be
digitally articulated. This is a method-level description inferred from
the source, not a validated psychological construct or a claim of
privileged access.

The source treats intuition as fallible, trainable, correctable, and
subordinate to constraint. Within the protocol, an intuitive candidate
earns continued attention only when iteration makes it more explicit, it
survives disconfirming pressure, and it transfers beyond the context
that first made it salient. Confidence should track those tests rather
than the intensity, familiarity, or felt depth of the intuition.

No single convergence measure is supplied by the source. Possible
evaluations include time to a testable candidate, survival under
independent review, prediction performance, and transfer to held-out
cases. These are proposed evaluation dimensions, not validated metrics,
and they can conflict: a rapidly convergent model may be narrow, while a
transferable model may require more cycles.

## **7. Residual Ambiguity and Incomplete Models**

The method rejects total closure as a default success criterion. Bounded
analysts rarely exhaust the possibility space of a sufficiently complex
problem; measurement and concepts may lag the structure under study; and
some systems remain uncertain because their states, interactions, or
future inputs cannot be specified completely. These claims do not
require an infinite or irreducibly unknowable reality.

Residual ambiguity can preserve learning capacity by marking what is
unknown, preventing a useful local model from becoming a universal
story, and identifying future tests. It can also become an excuse for
never rejecting a preferred idea. A residual should therefore record the
failed test or missing information that produced it, the decision it
could change, and the condition under which it will be revisited.

- Unresolved exception: a case that the current rule does not explain.

- Missing variable: a factor implied by the pattern but not yet
  represented or measurable.

- Definition conflict: a term changes sense across stages or
  stakeholders.

- Constraint conflict: two supported requirements cannot both be
  satisfied in the current design.

- Resource-bounded remainder: a question deferred because testing cost
  exceeds the present decision's value.

These categories are editorial operationalizations of the source's
residual concept. They make incompleteness auditable without treating
every remainder as equally informative.

## **8. Narrative Displacement Index**

### **8.1 Proposed metric**

The source proposes the Narrative Displacement Index (NDI) to detect
responsibility escape into abstractions such as 'the system,' 'culture,'
'society,' 'the algorithm,' or 'God.' The measure is not validated and
should not be used as a score of truth, guilt, motive, or organizational
health.

**NDI (source metric with editorial operationalization).** For a defined
sample of consequential explanatory statements, count abstract
attributions A and agent- or role-specific attributions L. Report A, L,
and the source ratio A/L when L is greater than zero. When L equals
zero, report 'no localized attribution' rather than an infinite score.
No threshold or normative cutoff is established.

Coding must distinguish an abstract label used as shorthand for named
mechanisms from an abstraction that replaces those mechanisms. 'The
algorithm ranked the item' may be a testable technical statement if the
model, data flow, deployment decision, and operator roles can be
examined. Conversely, a sentence naming a person can still displace
responsibility if it ignores authorization, coercion, or distributed
implementation. Reliability would require a codebook, independent
coders, agreement checks, a defined unit of analysis, and comparison
against outcomes not supplied in the source.

### **8.2 Hypotheses and limits**

The source predicts that high narrative displacement accompanies
scapegoating, moral laundering, escalation, and eventual coordination
breakdown. These are hypotheses. The same high abstract-attribution rate
may appear in legitimate system analysis, early-stage inquiry,
privacy-preserving reporting, or contexts where records do not permit
safe localization. NDI should therefore trigger a request for an
authorship map, not a conclusion about blame.

The proposed intervention is sequential: restore the difference between
constraint context and event-level authorship; identify responsible
roles and evidence; then reform enabling conditions without dissolving
the contributions of agents who selected, maintained, or could correct
the outcome.

## **9. Application Templates**

### **9.1 Organizational and leadership failure**

A narrative such as 'the CEO caused everything' may be an unsupported
scapegoat, a compressed but partially accurate account, or a claim about
policy authorship and oversight. The method does not decide among these
possibilities by definition. It maps policies, incentives, reporting
lines, delegated authority, implementation decisions, notice, and
correction capacity. Senior leadership can bear responsibility for
direct acts, authorization, incentives, negligent oversight,
concealment, or continued maintenance even when managers and teams also
author local acts.

### **9.2 Family and relationship dynamics**

Family structure can shape roles, taboo topics, alliances, dependencies,
and the cost of disclosure. Individuals can also author punishment,
deception, exclusion, coercion, or triangulation. The protocol rejects
both 'the child is the problem' and 'the system made every act
inevitable' when either statement outruns the evidence. It separates
enabling histories and current pressures from observable acts, then asks
what protection, repair, boundary, or accountability process is
available. This is an analytic template, not a clinical assessment tool.

### **9.3 Workplace conflict**

The source describes a high-integrity, rule-following worker who resists
informal social games and becomes a threat node. Integrity and threat
cannot be inferred from self-description or conflict alone. The method
instead tests whether expectations were explicit, role-relevant conduct
was affected, standards were applied consistently, retaliation or
exclusion occurred, and decision makers can state evidence and appeal
paths. Localizing a choice to criticize, exclude, or sanction does not
prejudge whether that choice was warranted.

### **9.4 AI and technical accountability**

In AI-mediated decisions, 'the algorithm did it' is incomplete when it
obscures choices about objectives, training data, evaluation,
deployment, thresholds, monitoring, user interface, and override
authority. The model can be causally relevant without possessing human
event-level authorship. The protocol records the technical mechanism
alongside the decisions of developers, deployers, operators, managers,
and users, while avoiding the opposite error of assigning every
downstream effect to a single designer.

Table 4. Application template

| **Domain** | **Constraint context** | **Authorship questions** | **Correction target** |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Organization | Policies, incentives, hierarchy, reporting and resource rules | Who designed, authorized, implemented, concealed, or failed to correct? | Change constraints and address supported responsibility at each role. |
| Family or relationship | History, dependency, roles, speech rules, material and safety conditions | Which observable acts were chosen, enabled, resisted, or repeated? | Protect, bound, repair, and hold accountable without erasing context. |
| Workplace conflict | Role expectations, informal norms, evaluation and appeal procedures | Who interpreted, documented, sanctioned, retaliated, or reviewed? | Clarify standards and test consequential judgments against evidence. |
| AI-mediated decision | Model, data, interface, deployment, monitoring and override design | Who selected objectives, thresholds, use context, and response? | Correct technical mechanisms and governance decision points together. |

## **10. Practitioner Protocol**

### **10.1 Setup checklist**

- Define the decision domain, users, time horizon, and exclusions.

- State the attribution invariant: enablement alone does not establish
  event-level authorship.

- Collect non-negotiable facts, commitments, definitions, and evidence
  limits.

- Identify consequential decision points where selection, authorization,
  execution, maintenance, or correction may occur.

- Choose a stopping rule and specify what level of uncertainty the
  decision can tolerate.

### **10.2 Run the loop**

1.  Analog expansion. Gather signals, candidate structures, metaphors,
    and weak relations; mark confidence as provisional.

2.  Digital constraint. Translate candidates into explicit claims, test
    facts and logic, compare alternatives, and enforce responsibility
    localization.

3.  Residual analysis. Record what remains unresolved, why it survived,
    and what observation or decision it could change.

4.  Iteration. Revise the search surface and continue until the declared
    stability or stopping condition is met.

### **10.3 Proposed implementation artifacts**

**Candidate ledger.** A list of analog candidates, their source,
confidence, and the digital test each must face.

**Constraint register.** A record of facts, definitions, commitments,
exclusions, and evidence limits used to prune candidates.

**Authorship map.** A decision-point record distinguishing context,
selection, authorization, execution, maintenance, omission, and
correction authority.

**Residual log.** A record of exceptions, missing variables, definition
conflicts, constraint conflicts, and resource-bounded remainders that
may guide later cycles.

These artifacts are editorial operationalizations derived from the
source protocol. They are proposed implementation aids, not established
components of a validated method.

### **10.4 Immediate reset conditions**

- An unfalsifiable claim is protected by adding exceptions after each
  failed test.

- Agency is displaced upward into an abstraction without a
  decision-point analysis.

- A system label is treated as a choosing actor or, conversely, as
  causally irrelevant by definition.

- A symbolic or linguistic relation is treated as proof rather than a
  search heuristic.

- Closure is forced without testing alternative explanations or relevant
  cases.

- Residuals accumulate without prioritization, testing conditions, or a
  stopping rule.

## **11. Proposed Evaluation and Predictions**

### **11.1 Evaluation dimensions**

A method that claims to accelerate discovery and preserve agency should
be evaluated against alternatives. The following dimensions are proposed
metrics or measurement targets, not validated scales:

- Novelty yield: the number and quality of testable candidates not
  represented in the initial parameter set.

- Constraint survival: the proportion of candidates retained after
  specified logical, empirical, and alternative-explanation tests.

- Residual utility: the proportion of logged residuals that later
  produce a testable revision or decision-relevant distinction.

- Transfer performance: performance of the resulting model on cases not
  used to generate it.

- Attribution completeness: the proportion of consequential decisions
  with identified context, responsible roles, evidence, and correction
  authority.

- Narrative displacement: raw abstract and localized attribution counts,
  plus the conditional NDI ratio described in Section 8.

Each measure requires a unit of analysis, baseline, coding protocol, and
cost model. Maximizing one measure can damage another. More analog
candidate generation can increase novelty while lowering efficiency;
stronger digital pruning can improve apparent survival while excluding
unfamiliar but valuable candidates.

### **11.2 Testable predictions**

1.  For underparameterized tasks, a staged analog-digital process will
    generate more later-testable candidate structures than an early
    fixed-variable process under comparable time and expertise.

2.  Unconstrained analog exploration will produce more candidate
    associations but a lower proportion that survive independent
    constraint testing than the cyclic protocol.

3.  Explicit residual logging will increase the rate at which failed
    tests produce a revised testable hypothesis compared with recording
    only accepted and rejected conclusions.

4.  Dual attribution will produce more identifiable correction targets
    than explanations using only abstract structural labels or only
    individual blame.

5.  Higher narrative displacement will be associated with weaker
    agreement about decision ownership and correction pathways, but not
    necessarily with greater wrongdoing or worse outcomes.

These predictions require preregistered tasks or coding rules, credible
comparison methods, independent evaluation, and safeguards against
defining success as agreement with the method's preferred language.
Failure of a prediction should constrain the relevant mechanism rather
than be recoded as a meaningful residual by default.

## **12. Scope, Failure Modes, and Metaphysical Use**

### **12.1 Scope limits**

The framework does not:

- establish a general scientific theory of discovery or causation;

- prove that analog reasoning is superior in every domain or phase;

- treat navigational coherence as empirical truth, moral goodness, or
  consensus;

- make constraints, institutions, algorithms, or metaphysical structures
  causally irrelevant;

- erase responsibilities attached to design, authorization, supervision,
  maintenance, or omission;

- infer innocence, guilt, intent, coercion, or clinical status from an
  attribution map or NDI score;

- permit a failed test to be preserved indefinitely merely by calling it
  a residual; or

- replace domain expertise, empirical research, legal standards,
  clinical assessment, or case-specific evidence.

### **12.2 Metaphysical hygiene**

The source applies the attribution invariant to metaphysical modeling
and calls the result metaphysical hygiene. In this paper that use is a
model-internal proposal: a metaphysical structure may be represented as
setting possibility conditions without being treated as the event-level
author of every action. The distinction does not validate the structure,
establish divine or cosmic roles, or show that metaphysical language is
necessary for the protocol.

Analog metaphysical language can therefore guide questions only if it is
tagged as analogy or proposal and later translated into claims that can
be evaluated at the appropriate level. Analogy cannot function as
evidence, and uncertainty about ultimate conditions cannot be used to
displace responsibility for traceable human decisions.

### **12.3 Method-level failure modes**

- Analog capture: a compelling association becomes identity-protected
  before testing.

- Digital ossification: available variables become the boundary of what
  participants are allowed to notice.

- Residual inflation: every anomaly is preserved, preventing cumulative
  rejection.

- Attribution laundering: the invariant is used to shield designers or
  leaders whose contributions are traceable.

- Local-blame collapse: contextual pressure is ignored because an
  individual action can be identified.

- Coherence substitution: internal consistency is treated as proof that
  the method found reality.

## **13. What the Method Produces**

When it functions as intended, Analog-Digital Coherence Navigation
produces a framework rather than a final theory: a candidate map, a
constraint-tested model, an explicit residual log, and a responsibility
map tied to correction pathways. Analog search makes unparameterized
relations discoverable; digital testing makes retention contestable;
residual analysis keeps incompleteness available for later work; and
dual attribution keeps contextual reform connected to local and
design-level responsibility.

The method's strongest promise is procedural modesty. It seeks models
stable enough to navigate while preserving the evidence, alternatives,
and unresolved surfaces needed for revision. Whether it accelerates
discovery, improves accountability, or transfers across domains remains
an empirical question.

## **One-Sentence Summary**

Analog-Digital Coherence Navigation is a discipline for crossing the
seam between an idea that isn't yet fully formed and a claim that can be
tested, without letting either side of that seam quietly absorb
responsibility that belongs to a traceable decision. The next paper in
this axis (VII.04) narrows this same discipline to language itself,
modeling how a speaker shifts among neutral, functional, and
boundary-defensive modes as a control layer under social pressure.

# Conclusion

“Crossing the Seam Without Tearing Reality”

Analog-Digital Coherence Navigation formalizes a common but often hidden
feature of inquiry: discovery begins with relations that are not yet
fully articulable, while reliable use requires those relations to face
explicit constraint. Alternating expansion and testing can preserve
novelty without granting intuition authority. Residual analysis can turn
failed or incomplete tests into bounded guidance without making
incompleteness unfalsifiable.

The method's attribution discipline is equally important. Constraint
context and event-level authorship answer different questions and should
be analyzed together. Enablement alone does not establish who selected
an act, but contextual explanation is not exculpation and local
authorship is not the only responsibility-bearing role. Properly
limited, blame immunization protects an inquiry from recursive
misattribution while preserving warranted accountability. The result is
a proposed infrastructure for directional closure: enough coherence to
act, enough residual visibility to revise, and enough causal precision
to keep responsibility attached to evidence.

## **Key Contributions**

1.  A four-phase cyclic protocol linking analog expansion, digital
    constraint, residual analysis, and iteration.

2.  A calibrated attribution invariant separating formal enablement from
    event-level authorship without denying structural causal relevance.

3.  A dual-attribution method that maps constraint context alongside
    selection, authorization, execution, maintenance, omission, and
    correction roles.

4.  A proposed Narrative Displacement Index with explicit coding,
    denominator, validation, and interpretation limits.

5.  Operational guardrails, artifacts, applications, metrics,
    predictions, and failure modes that keep the method corrigible
    rather than self-sealing.
