# Abstract

“Staying Whole Under Watch”

Where the previous paper diagnosed how reference-hoarding curdles into
identity-fused half-knowledge and a cult logic that resists correction,
this one turns to the pressure that keeps such fusions from ever
resolving. This paper develops an agnostic account of conflicts
historically described as spiritual war, and it locates that conflict in
sustained pressure on internal coherence: the continuity of values,
interpretations, boundaries, and self-concept under social observation.
The model distinguishes ordinary disagreement from attempts to induce
self-contradiction once difference can no longer be assimilated or
safely ignored, and a companion concept—structural hygiene—separates
invariant constraints, local agency, and narrative compression so that
metaphysical language does not silently become causal attribution. The
paper preserves the experiential precision of spiritual language without
requiring supernatural claims, and it adds safeguards against treating
disagreement, criticism, mental illness, or ordinary influence as
manipulation. Internal coherence is not evidence of truth or virtue; the
practical objective is correction-safe autonomy, meaning enough
stability to revise beliefs without coerced identity fracture.

## **1. Problem and Scope**

Spiritual-war language persists because it identifies a real domain of
conflict: conscience, meaning, identity, and self-trust. Secular
translation can clarify the mechanisms underneath that language, but it
can also truncate the experience itself, whether by reducing every
conflict to symptoms or by taking every spiritual description as a
literal claim about entities.

The framework offered here is descriptive and agnostic. It neither
affirms nor denies metaphysical interpretations; instead it asks a
narrower question, which is when social pressure shifts from argument
about claims or behavior toward non-consensual destabilization of a
person's capacity to interpret and revise their own experience.

## **2. Definitions**

**Internal coherence.** Relative consistency among an individual's
values, interpretations, boundaries, and self-concept across time.
Coherence does not imply accuracy, goodness, or immunity to revision.

**Social observation.** Interpersonal, familial, institutional,
professional, or mediated contexts in which identity and behavior are
interpreted and responded to.

**Manipulation.** Pressure that seeks to alter another person's
interpretation or action while obscuring relevant intent, evidence,
alternatives, or exit. The term requires more than disagreement or
persuasion.

**Narrative compression.** A symbolic account that makes experience
communicable by omitting detail. Narratives can guide action without
being identical to the underlying structure they compress.

**Structural hygiene.** A discipline of keeping constraint, causation,
agency, narrative, evidence, and metaphor from being substituted for one
another.

**Correction-safe autonomy.** The capacity to preserve self-authorship
while remaining open to evidence, consent-based influence, and
non-punitive revision.

## **3. Three-Phase Mechanism**

1.  **Stable internal organization.** The person maintains a workable
    self-model, boundaries, and interpretive independence without
    requiring others to agree.

2.  **Non-assimilation becomes salient.** Surrounding actors or systems
    experience the person's difference as difficult to categorize,
    mirror, govern, or absorb.

3.  **Pressure substitutes for coexistence.** Reframing, guilt, fear,
    pathologization, or narrative substitution targets the person's
    authority over internal meaning.

This sequence is a proposed pattern, not a presumption of motive, since
similar behavior can arise from misunderstanding, care, institutional
duty, cultural mismatch, or reciprocal escalation. Attribution requires
evidence about persistence, consent, power, exit, and response to
correction.

## **4. Disagreement, Influence, and Destabilization**

Table 1. Distinguishing related interactions

| **Interaction** | **Typical features** | **Correction test** |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Good-faith disagreement | Claims and evidence remain contestable; difference may persist | Both parties can restate, revise, or disengage |
| Consensual influence | Persuasion is visible and alternatives remain available | Consent can be withdrawn without identity punishment |
| Protective intervention | Concern centers on observable risk or impairment | Evidence, proportionality, review, and due process remain available |
| Identity destabilization | Pressure targets self-trust or interpretive autonomy beyond the disputed claim | Exit and correction are blocked, punished, or recursively reframed |

## **5. Why Spiritual Language Persists**

The word spiritual can preserve the felt location of a conflict even
when its ontology is disputed, since it signals that the pressure
concerns meaning, conscience, belonging, and identity rather than only
visible conduct. The language is useful when held as phenomenological
description; it becomes risky when metaphor is converted into proof of
hidden entities, coordinated enemies, or moral superiority.

A structurally hygienic translation can therefore preserve both
vocabularies at once: the experience may be described spiritually, while
causal claims remain subject to ordinary evidentiary standards.

## **6. Structure, Agency, and Narrative**

Table 2. Layers that structural hygiene keeps distinct

| **Layer** | **Role** | **Common substitution** |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Constraint | Defines or changes possible action | Treated as an intentional actor |
| Causation | Contributes to an event or outcome | Collapsed into blame or moral agency |
| Agency | Selects, authorizes, maintains, revises, or refuses | Exported upward to history, systems, algorithms, or divinity |
| Narrative | Compresses experience into communicable meaning | Mistaken for complete structure or dismissed as meaningless |
| Metaphor | Transfers relations across domains | Presented as literal mechanism or evidence |

The precursor or creation role used elsewhere in the manuscript should
be treated as a proposed structural referent, since enabling conditions
are not event-level authors. Denying metaphysical agency does not
require assigning total agency to history, biology, capital, algorithms,
or institutions instead.

## **7. Language Failure and Metaphysical Drift**

Discrete language compresses gradients into categories such as
exists/does not exist, voluntary/coerced, healthy/pathological, or
aligned/misaligned. These binaries can clarify decisions, but they can
also conceal mixed causation, partial agency, and uncertainty.

Metaphysical drift is not automatically proof that a model has revealed
an invariant; it may instead reflect ambiguity, rhetorical priming,
cultural inheritance, or an underdefined term. Structural hygiene
requires the model to disclose those alternatives rather than treating
misinterpretation as confirmation.

## **8. Longitudinal and Abstracted Harm**

Pressure on internal coherence can accumulate through repeated
reframing, inherited narratives, inconsistent rules, and punishment of
correction, so harm may be distributed across many low-intensity
interactions rather than arriving as a single overt event. Duration and
recurrence matter more than any one isolated disagreement, and power,
dependency, and exit cost all affect whether influence is meaningfully
voluntary in the first place. Observable effects should be kept separate
from assumptions about intent, not least because targets may themselves
misread care, criticism, or plain incompatibility as manipulation;
independent records and third-party review help reduce exactly these
recursive disputes about what actually happened.

## **9. Structural Hygiene Protocol**

1.  **Name the dispute precisely.** State the disputed claim, behavior,
    boundary, or decision without vague framing.

2.  **Separate layers of description.** Distinguish observed events from
    interpretations, metaphors, and inferred motives.

3.  **Map the leverage in play.** Identify power, dependency,
    information, alternatives, and exit costs on each side.

4.  **Test for identity punishment.** Check whether disagreement can
    persist without triggering identity-level retaliation.

5.  **Check for available review.** Confirm whether each side can
    revise, pause, or seek independent review.

6.  **Prefer the least loaded explanation.** Use the least
    metaphysically loaded account that still preserves the relevant
    experience.

7.  **Retain honest uncertainty.** Where evidence cannot distinguish
    manipulation from mismatch or reciprocal escalation, say so rather
    than forcing a verdict.

## **10. Alignment Without Belief Enforcement**

Alignment is defined here provisionally as coherent navigation of
constraints, not doctrinal obedience, which is why people with
incompatible metaphysical narratives can still act responsibly,
compassionately, and predictably alongside one another. Shared action
does not require agreement about the ultimate source of constraint.

The stronger claim, that coherence automatically reduces friction or
produces moral convergence, remains a hypothesis rather than a settled
result. Coherent actors may still pursue incompatible goals, and
internally coherent systems can still be harmful, so outcomes, consent,
reciprocity, and correction remain the necessary tests.

## **11. Revelation, Iteration, and Detractors**

The source reframes revelation as local recognition of a pre-existing
pattern rather than supernatural interruption. Used cautiously, this can
describe a compression event in which ambiguity falls enough to make
coordinated action possible, though it does not establish that the
recognized pattern is true, universal, or historically inevitable.

1.  **Constraint or pattern recognition.** A regularity becomes visible
    enough to name.

2.  **Local experimentation and provisional alignment.** Individuals
    test the pattern against their own experience.

3.  **Coordination amplification.** Shared recognition lets separate
    actors act together.

4.  **Narrative formation.** The recognized pattern is compressed into a
    communicable story.

5.  **Compression drift and institutionalization.** The story hardens
    into doctrine as it spreads.

6.  **Renewed recognition, correction, or fork.** The cycle either
    self-corrects or splits.

Detractors are expected in plural systems because language remains
lossy, alignment is voluntary, and learning is asynchronous. Opposition
can also identify genuine errors, so a system is not resilient because
criticism cannot break it; it is resilient when criticism can be
processed without coercive convergence or total collapse.

## **12. Boundaries and Non-Examples**

This model is not meant to cover every instance of interpersonal
friction, and several ordinary categories fall outside it entirely:
voluntary belief change or ordinary self-doubt, good-faith criticism
supported by reasons and evidence, mutual influence with revocable
consent, clinical concern evaluated through appropriate evidence and
safeguards, consequences for harmful behavior that preserve due process,
and temporary conflict or incompatibility where disengagement remains
available. The framework must not be used to dismiss all criticism as
identity attack, to replace clinical assessment, or to certify the
narrator's account without corroboration.

## **13. Applications**

Table 3. Practical applications

| **Context** | **Use** | **Safeguard** |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Families and relationships | Distinguish disagreement from recursive pressure on self-trust | Attend to mutual escalation and safety |
| Institutions | Audit whether correction and dissent are possible without identity penalties | Separate performance standards from conformity demands |
| Clinical and support settings | Preserve lived meaning while evaluating risk and impairment | Do not substitute this model for professional assessment |
| Plural communities | Coordinate through actions and interfaces despite narrative difference | Protect exit, translation, and revision |

## **14. Limits and Research Needs**

Several limits bound how far this model should be trusted. Internal
coherence is difficult to measure and can coexist with false beliefs;
accounts of manipulation are vulnerable to asymmetric information and
reciprocal attribution; cultural norms differ in how autonomy, family
obligation, spirituality, and disagreement are expressed; the
three-phase mechanism still requires empirical comparison with ordinary
conflict and coercive-control models; and claims about civilizational
phases, recurring revelation, and moral convergence remain theoretical
rather than demonstrated. None of these open questions change the basic
shape of the model, but they do mark where the next paper picks up:
VII.08 turns from pressure on the self toward the metaphysics of who, or
what, could be composing the constraints that apply that pressure in the
first place.

# Conclusion

“Staying Whole Under Watch”

Internal-coherence conflict names a real class of experiences without
deciding their metaphysical interpretation in advance. The model becomes
useful when it identifies persistent pressure on self-authorship,
blocked exit, and substitution of manipulation for claim-level
engagement.

Structural hygiene keeps that insight from becoming another closed
narrative. It separates observation from interpretation, constraint from
agency, metaphor from mechanism, and coherence from truth. The aim is
not invulnerability to influence; it is correction-safe autonomy, the
ability to remain a person while learning, disagreeing, revising, or
leaving.

## **Key Contributions**

- **An agnostic model.** A three-phase account of pressure on internal
  coherence that takes no position on metaphysical interpretation.

- **A working distinction.** A way to separate disagreement, consensual
  influence, protective intervention, and identity destabilization from
  one another.

- **A structural-hygiene protocol.** A discipline for keeping
  constraint, causation, agency, narrative, and metaphor from collapsing
  into each other.

- **A qualified account of spiritual language.** Treatment of revelation
  and recurring narrative cycles that neither affirms nor dismisses
  their content.

- **Explicit safeguards.** Guardrails against metaphysical inflation,
  criticism refusal, clinical substitution, and one-sided attribution.
