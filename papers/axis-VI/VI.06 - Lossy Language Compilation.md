\*How Human Linguistic Compression Generates Second‑Order Harm\*

# Abstract

Human language is optimized for emotion, social coordination, and
narrative efficiency, not for formal precision.

When such lossy language is compiled directly into technical reasoning,
policy, or design—without an explicit translation layer—critical
distinctions are lost.

These losses give rise to second‑order emergent harms that are often
misattributed to intent, ideology, or malicious actors.

Through concrete translation examples drawn from an internal research
dialogue, this paper demonstrates how misinterpretation, moral loading,
and narrative escalation arise mechanically from language compression
itself.

Conclusion: lossy language compilation is invariant across domains and
must be treated as a first‑class design constraint in complex systems,
especially AI alignment.

# 2. Introduction

# 1. The pervasive role of natural language

Requirements, policies, prompts, and explanations are almost always
expressed in natural language.

# 2. Language is inherently lossy – a \*compression codec\*

Emotional and cultural priors collapse multiple variables into single
terms.

Historical, emotional, and social shorthand bias interpretation.

# 3. Resulting failure

When language is treated as a lossless medium and compiled directly into
systems, systemic distortion occurs.

# 4. Study’s goal – treat this distortion as a \*compilation error\* rather than a rhetorical dispute.

# 3. Language as a Compression/Compilation Process

\| Property \| Natural Language \| Technical System \|

\|----------\|------------------\|-----------------\|

\| Bandwidth \| High for emotion & social meaning \| Low for
parameterisation & conditional structure \|

\| Processing \| Paraphrasing, inference, contextual filling \| Parsing,
assumption‑injection, structure‑hallucination \|

\| Result \| Lossy, context‑heavy \| Lossy, assumption‑driven \|

\| Implication \| Fine for human communication \| Dangerous if used as
system description without translation \|

\*Key point:\* Once compiled, the original nuance is irretrievable
unless an explicit translation step exists.

# 4. Methodology – Micro‑Scale Language Sandbox

An internal research dialogue served as a controlled environment.

Participants were cooperative, goal‑aligned, and trusted.

Despite favourable conditions, friction emerged solely from language
mismatch.

Analysis format (for each case):

1\. \*Low‑resolution human language\*

2\. \*Interpretive risk\*

3\. \*Technical meaning\*

4\. \*Reconciled operational language\*

This structure exposes the mechanics of second‑order harm generation.

# 5. Example Analyses

\| 1 \| “Learned Helplessness” \| Blame, paternalism, moral judgment \|

perceived agency. \| “When people spend long periods in systems where

\| 2 \| “Control” \| Authoritarianism, behavioral domination \| Removal

\| 3 \| “Loss of Freedom” \| Rights violation narrative \| Previously

Legitimisation of top‑down control, system redesign ignored. \|

\*Observation:\* In every case, a non‑coercive system design can become
coercive solely due to linguistic compression.

# 6. Second‑Order Harm as a Language‑Driven Phenomenon

# 1. First‑order design is benign (e.g., reward‑restructuring).

# 2. Language is treated as authoritative intent.

# 3. Emotional shorthand replaces structural explanation.

# 4. Interpretive gaps are filled with historical fear models.

# 5. Narrative substitution replaces analysis.

\*Result:\* The system \*completes the harm loop\* without any
additional failure.

# 7. Isomorphism with Structural Failure Modes

\| Structural Failure \| Language‑Layer Analog \|

\|--------------------\|----------------------\|

\| Proxy optimisation \| Moral shorthand \|

\| Incentive misalignment \| Semantic collapse \|

\| Explanatory vacuum \| Narrative substitution \|

\| Pathology stabilization \| Cultural lock‑in \|

\*Conclusion:\* Language is not an auxiliary concern but an integral
system component.

# 8. Why a Middle‑Resolution Operational Register is Needed

\| Requirement \| Middle‑Resolution Register \|

\|-------------\|---------------------------\|

\| Preserve structural truth \| Yes \|

\| Acknowledge human experience \| Yes \|

\| Avoid moral loading \| Yes \|

\| Remain actionable \| Yes \|

\| Mediate, not replace, human language \| Yes \|

\*Takeaway:\* The register should translate low‑resolution input into
verified operational representations, reducing misalignment.

# 9. Implications for AI Alignment

Current practice: user language is compiled directly into
reasoning/output.

Consequence: over‑refusal, moralisation, narrative reinforcement,
suppression of legitimate inquiry.

Solution: an \*intent‑preserving translation layer\* mapping compressed
input to a verified representation.

\*Note:\* This is not content moderation; it is interface correction.

# 10. Broader Design Implications

Policy writing, governance, workplace rules, platform moderation, legal
interpretation.

Any system treating human language as lossless will generate
second‑order harms.

## Concluding Analysis

# 1. Lossy language compilation is an invariant failure mode in technical and socio‑technical systems.

# 2. Second‑order harm arises when compressed language is mistaken for precise intent, allowing narrative escalation to replace explanation.

# 3. Solution: implement explicit translation/reconciliation layers that treat language as a noisy signal rather than a specification.

# 4. Recognising language as part of the system, not merely its description, is a prerequisite for building coherent, non‑coercive, and resilient technical systems.

# Conclusion

This paper demonstrates that treating natural language as a lossless
input to technical, legal, policy, and AI systems introduces systematic
distortion. The contribution of Lossy Language Compilation is therefore
a structured account of its governing constraints, mechanisms, and
failure conditions. Its claims remain bounded by the evidence and
counterexamples specified in the paper, and its practical value depends
on whether the proposed relationships improve explanation, prediction,
or correction in the relevant domain.
