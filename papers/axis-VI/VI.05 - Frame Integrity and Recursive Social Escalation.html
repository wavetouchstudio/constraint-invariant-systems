A Control-Theoretic Account of Misinterpreted Clarification and Runaway Interaction Effects

Abstract

“Frames Colliding at Full Speed”

VI.04 traced an asymmetry in how much scrutiny a claim receives depending on which frame it's read in; this paper zooms into a single live channel where two frames collide directly and watches the collision escalate on its own. It analyzes a recurring communication failure in mixed-context social environments: when a speaker preserves frame integrity through functional clarification, listeners operating under default social frames may interpret repeated clarification as dominance, aggression, or hostility. Over time, this misinterpretation can generate runaway social escalation—resentment, exclusion, and the attribution of negative character traits to the speaker. The phenomenon is formalised as a frame-control collision between epistemic and social norms, its recursive dynamics are described, and the disproportionate impact on high-precision or atypical communication profiles (including many autistic speakers) is explained. The paper concludes with implications for discourse design, group dynamics, and institutional communication.

Introduction

Conversation relies on implicit agreements about the type of activity occurring, and these agreements are rarely explicit—they are inferred from tone, convention, and social norms. Problems arise when participants operate under different framing assumptions: one party treats language as a functional instrument for modeling mechanisms, while others treat language as a social instrument for managing identity, status, and affiliation. The failure mode this paper examines is that when a speaker clarifies their own intended frame in response to misinterpretation, that clarification itself can be interpreted as a social threat, leading to recursive escalation—a pattern often mislabeled as rudeness, intensity, condescension, or bullying, despite the speaker's consistent good-faith intent.

Frame Integrity

Definition: the speaker's effort to preserve the original meaning and scope of their statement against reinterpretation. Its components are:

  1. Functional definition of terms.

  2. Rejection of moral or identity-based readings.

  3. Post-misinterpretation clarification that restores the intended domain.

  4. Maintenance of the original model rather than adaptation to social expectations.

Role: a stability strategy in epistemic communication.

Why It Runs Away

The speaker's corrections stay consistent while the listener's social frame persists, and social systems penalise repeated correction regardless of accuracy. Over time, evaluation shifts from what is said to who keeps correcting—which is the origin of the "runaway" effect.

Disproportionate Impact on High-Precision Communicators

High-Cognition Profiles

High-cognition communicators think in models or abstractions, treat ambiguity as error, and prioritize correctness over harmony—which makes them more likely to maintain frame integrity under pressure, and so more likely to trigger recursion.

Autistic Communication Profiles

Autistic communicators often use words literally or functionally, do not rely on implied social meaning, correct misunderstandings directly, and resist softening language that changes meaning. Frequently placed in environments where social frames dominate, this style gets misread as hostility, with potential bullying or exclusion as a result. Conclusion: the bullying or exclusion originates from systematic frame mismatch combined with social intolerance for repeated correction, not from autism itself.

Concluding Analysis

When speakers clarify their own frame to preserve meaning, and listeners reinterpret that clarification through social norms, a recursive escalation can emerge. Over time, the pattern is mislabeled as aggression or bullying, even though it originates in good-faith communication. This dynamic explains why autistic individuals and other high-precision communicators are frequently targeted or excluded—not because of what they say, but because they must continually defend their frame in environments that treat such defense as a social violation. Understanding this mechanism reframes the issue from one of personality or pathology to one of incompatible control systems operating in the same conversational space.

Key Contributions

  1. Formalisation of the frame-control collision between epistemic and social norms.

  2. Recursive dynamics and their escalation mechanism.

  3. Identification of high-precision and autistic profiles as disproportionately impacted.

  4. Practical guidelines for reducing escalation in individual, group, and AI-mediated communication.

Conclusion

“Frames Colliding at Full Speed”

This paper presents a control-theoretic account of how repeated clarification—intended to preserve epistemic accuracy—can trigger social escalation when listeners operate under default harmony-oriented norms. The contribution of Frame Integrity and Recursive Social Escalation is therefore a structured account of its governing constraints, mechanisms, and failure conditions. Its claims remain bounded by the evidence and counterexamples specified in the paper, and its practical value depends on whether the proposed relationships improve explanation, prediction, or correction in the relevant domain. The next paper (VI.06) moves from social frame collisions to the language itself, asking what gets lost whenever meaning is compiled down into words at all.
