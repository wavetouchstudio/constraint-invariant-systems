*A Control‑Theoretic Account of Misinterpreted Clarification and Runaway Interaction Effects*

Abstract

“Frames Colliding at Full Speed”

This paper analyzes a recurring communication failure in mixed‑context social environments: when a speaker preserves *frame integrity* through functional clarification, listeners operating under default social frames may interpret repeated clarification as dominance, aggression, or hostility. Over time, this misinterpretation can generate runaway social escalation—resentment, exclusion, and the attribution of negative character traits to the speaker. The phenomenon is formalised as a frame‑control collision between epistemic and social norms, its recursive dynamics are described, and the disproportionate impact on high‑precision or atypical communication profiles (including many autistic speakers) is explained. The paper concludes with implications for discourse design, group dynamics, and institutional communication.

1. Introduction

“Frames Colliding at Full Speed”

Conversation relies on implicit agreements about the *type* of activity occurring.

These agreements are rarely explicit; they are inferred from tone, convention, and social norms.

Problems arise when participants operate under different framing assumptions:

One party treats language as a *functional instrument* for modeling mechanisms.

Others treat language as a *social instrument* for managing identity, status, and affiliation.

Failure mode examined:

> When a speaker clarifies their own intended frame in response to misinterpretation, that clarification itself can be interpreted as a social threat, leading to recursive escalation.

> This is often mislabeled as rudeness, intensity, condescension, or bullying, despite the speaker’s consistent good‑faith intent.

4.2 Why It Runs Away

Speaker’s corrections are consistent; listener’s social frame persists.

Social systems penalise repeated correction regardless of accuracy.

Over time, evaluation shifts from *what is said* to *who keeps correcting*.

Origin of “runaway” effects.

6. Disproportionate Impact on High‑Precision Communicators

“Frames Colliding at Full Speed”

6.1 High‑Cognition Profiles

Think in models or abstractions.

Treat ambiguity as error.

Prioritize correctness over harmony.

Likely to maintain frame integrity under pressure → higher chance of triggering recursion.

6.2 Autistic Communication Profiles

Use words literally or functionally.

Do not rely on implied social meaning.

Correct misunderstandings directly.

Resist softening language that changes meaning.

Frequently placed in environments where social frames dominate → misread as hostility → potential bullying or exclusion.

*Conclusion:* The bullying or exclusion originates from *systematic frame mismatch* combined with social intolerance for repeated correction, not from autism itself.

Concluding Analysis

When speakers clarify their own frame to preserve meaning, and listeners reinterpret that clarification through social norms, a recursive escalation can emerge. Over time, the pattern is mislabeled as aggression or bullying, even though it originates in good‑faith communication. This dynamic explains why autistic individuals and other high‑precision communicators are frequently targeted or excluded—not because of what they say, but because they must continually defend their frame in environments that treat such defense as a social violation. Understanding this mechanism reframes the issue from one of personality or pathology to one of incompatible control systems operating in the same conversational space.
