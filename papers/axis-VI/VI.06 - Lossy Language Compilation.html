How Human Linguistic Compression Generates Second-Order Harm

Abstract

“What Gets Lost Every Time You Explain Yourself”

VI.05 showed two frames colliding in a live social channel; this paper asks what happens even earlier, at the level of the words themselves. Human language is optimized for emotion, social coordination, and narrative efficiency, not for formal precision, so when such lossy language is compiled directly into technical reasoning, policy, or design—without an explicit translation layer—critical distinctions are lost. These losses give rise to second-order emergent harms that are often misattributed to intent, ideology, or malicious actors. Through concrete translation examples drawn from an internal research dialogue, this paper demonstrates how misinterpretation, moral loading, and narrative escalation arise mechanically from language compression itself. The conclusion: lossy language compilation is invariant across domains and must be treated as a first-class design constraint in complex systems, especially AI alignment.

Introduction

Requirements, policies, prompts, and explanations are almost always expressed in natural language, and natural language is inherently lossy—a compression codec in which emotional and cultural priors collapse multiple variables into single terms, and historical, emotional, and social shorthand bias interpretation. When language is treated as a lossless medium and compiled directly into systems, systemic distortion occurs. This study's goal is to treat that distortion as a compilation error rather than a rhetorical dispute.

Methodology — Micro-Scale Language Sandbox

An internal research dialogue served as a controlled environment: participants were cooperative, goal-aligned, and trusted, yet despite these favourable conditions, friction emerged solely from language mismatch. Each case is analyzed in four steps—the low-resolution human language used, the interpretive risk it carries, the technical meaning actually intended, and the reconciled operational language that resolves the gap—a structure that exposes the mechanics of second-order harm generation.

Second-Order Harm as a Language-Driven Phenomenon

The harm loop completes in five steps:

  1. First-order design is benign (e.g., reward-restructuring).

  2. Language is treated as authoritative intent.

  3. Emotional shorthand replaces structural explanation.

  4. Interpretive gaps are filled with historical fear models.

  5. Narrative substitution replaces analysis.

Result: the system completes the harm loop without any additional failure.

Implications for AI Alignment

Current practice compiles user language directly into reasoning and output, and the consequence is over-refusal, moralisation, narrative reinforcement, and suppression of legitimate inquiry. The solution is an intent-preserving translation layer mapping compressed input to a verified representation. Note: this is not content moderation; it is interface correction.

Broader Design Implications

The same pattern recurs across policy writing, governance, workplace rules, platform moderation, and legal interpretation—any system that treats human language as lossless will generate second-order harms.

Concluding Analysis

  1. Lossy language compilation is an invariant failure mode in technical and socio-technical systems.

  2. Second-order harm arises when compressed language is mistaken for precise intent, allowing narrative escalation to replace explanation.

  3. The solution is to implement explicit translation/reconciliation layers that treat language as a noisy signal rather than a specification.

  4. Recognising language as part of the system, not merely its description, is a prerequisite for building coherent, non-coercive, and resilient technical systems.

Conclusion

“What Gets Lost Every Time You Explain Yourself”

This paper demonstrates that treating natural language as a lossless input to technical, legal, policy, and AI systems introduces systematic distortion. The contribution of Lossy Language Compilation is therefore a structured account of its governing constraints, mechanisms, and failure conditions. Its claims remain bounded by the evidence and counterexamples specified in the paper, and its practical value depends on whether the proposed relationships improve explanation, prediction, or correction in the relevant domain. Having traced harm down to the level of compression in language, the next paper (VI.07) moves up a level again, to what an institution owes its members when procedural integrity and narrative safety start to pull in different directions.
