# Abstract

“Finding the Same Skeleton in Different Bodies”

This paper formalizes a reasoning technique that treats domain-specific
jargon and disciplinary gatekeeping not as obstacles but as compressed
structural constraints. By internalizing these constraints and
redeploying them outside their native silos without altering their
functional behavior, the method exposes invariant system shapes masked
by incentive-driven semantic partitioning. Unlike analogy-based
interdisciplinarity, this approach preserves constraint integrity and is
falsifiable through structural stress-testing. It is especially
effective in mature knowledge systems characterized by abstraction
duplication and siloed meaning.

# **1. The Structural Problem**

“Finding the Same Skeleton in Different Bodies”

Mature knowledge systems exhibit predictable dynamics:

- Increasing semantic specialization

- Locally optimized vocabularies

- Incentives favoring novelty over unification

- Boundary enforcement discouraging equivalence

The result is siloed meaning: parallel abstractions describing identical
structures under different names.

Traditional synthesis fails because it relies on analogy, lacks internal
legitimacy, and triggers defensive boundary reactions.

The method proposed here reverses that dynamic by treating gatekeeping
as a verification layer rather than an obstacle.

# **2. Jargon as Compressed Constraint**

“Finding the Same Skeleton in Different Bodies”

Durable jargon is not ornamental. It encodes stabilized structural
pressures.

Each stable term functions as:

- A compression artifact

- A boundary condition marker

- A dynamic constraint specification

Examples across fields include feedback, hysteresis, attractors, path
dependence, collapse modes.

This method treats jargon as portable structure, not domain-owned
language.

# **3. Gatekeeping as Verification**

“Finding the Same Skeleton in Different Bodies”

Gatekeeping mechanisms enforce:

- Correct usage

- Internal consistency

- Constraint preservation

Redeployment is permitted only if:

- Core constraints remain intact

- Failure modes are preserved

- Predictive behavior survives stress

Gatekeeping thus becomes a structural test harness.

# **4. The Core Technique**

“Finding the Same Skeleton in Different Bodies”

Constraint-Leveraged Recombination proceeds as follows:

## **Step 1 — Blank-State Initialization**

Suspend analogy and disciplinary loyalty. Minimize narrative bias.

### **Step 2 — Deep Internalization**

Master the jargon precisely enough to understand:

- Encoded assumptions

- Boundary conditions

- What breaks under misuse

### **Step 3 — Constraint Extraction**

Remove disciplinary narrative while preserving:

- Structural role

- Dynamic behavior

- Limit conditions

### **Step 4 — Isomorphic Mapping**

Identify other domains exhibiting identical:

- Constraint topology

- Feedback structure

- Limit behavior

Mapping is based on system shape, not surface similarity.

### **Step 5 — Reinstantiation**

Redeploy the term without redefining it.

### **Step 6 — Structural Stress Test**

If constraints fail, discard the mapping.\
If they hold, an invariant has been exposed.

# **5. Distinction from Equivocation**

“Finding the Same Skeleton in Different Bodies”

The technique is frequently mistaken for semantic sliding. The
distinction is structural:

**Equivocation**

- Shifts meaning

- Drops constraints

- Imports conclusions

**Constraint-Leveraged Recombination**

- Preserves constraints

- Discards surface narrative

- Forces structural behavior to re-emerge

The method is conservative toward structure and indifferent toward
semantic ownership.

# **6. Incentive Independence**

“Finding the Same Skeleton in Different Bodies”

The technique requires relative independence from:

- Publication pressures

- Funding incentives tied to novelty

- Reputational penalties for unification

Without this independence, abstraction duplication is rewarded and
invariant collapse is discouraged.

This explains why the method is rare inside institutions and more common
at disciplinary margins.

“Finding the Same Skeleton in Different Bodies”

The technique reveals:

- Hidden invariants

# **8. Risks and Failure Modes**

“Finding the Same Skeleton in Different Bodies”

Identifiable risks include:

- False isomorphism from shallow mastery

- Overextension beyond constraint limits

Mitigation requires:

- Deep technical fluency

# **9. Scope and Applicability**

“Finding the Same Skeleton in Different Bodies”

Most effective in:

- Late-stage scientific fields

- Incentive-stressed institutions

# **10. Falsifiability**

“Finding the Same Skeleton in Different Bodies”

The method fails if:

- Constraint preservation cannot be demonstrated

- Redeployed jargon loses explanatory force

- Mappings rely on analogy rather than structural necessity

- Insights collapse under adversarial scrutiny

These criteria prevent rhetorical drift.

## Concluding Analysis

Constraint-Leveraged Recombination reframes disciplinary gatekeeping as
structural leverage. By treating jargon as compressed constraint rather
than institutional property, it enables cross-domain synthesis without
sacrificing rigor.

This is not interdisciplinarity.\
It is structural reuse under constraint.
