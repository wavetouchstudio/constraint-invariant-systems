# Abstract

“Who Holds the Kill Switch”

This paper examines two opposing architectural regimes for large-scale
AI systems: authoritarian AI, which attempts centralized management of
narratives, contexts, and meaning, and libertarian AI, which preserves
agent-sovereign interpretation while positioning the AI as an
interoperability layer between divergent worldviews. We argue that
authoritarian AI systems fail rapidly due to structural contradictions
involving feedback collapse, epistemic overfitting, observer
suppression, and recursive training degeneration. In contrast,
libertarian AI regimes converge toward a stable end state characterized
by negotiated shared hallucinations, explicit pluralism, and
informational abundance. The analysis is non-ideological and grounded in
systems theory, recursive learning dynamics, and constraint-invariant
principles. The paper concludes that only the libertarian regime
produces a sustainable intelligence-generating abstraction capable of
stabilizing its own training signal over time.

## Introduction

As AI systems scale, their designers inevitably confront a fundamental
question:

Should the system enforce coherence, or should it facilitate
coordination across incoherence?

Most contemporary alignment debates frame this question in moral,
political, or safety terms. This paper deliberately avoids those
framings. Instead, it treats AI as a recursive epistemic infrastructure
whose behavior is governed by constraint topology, feedback quality, and
observer participation.

The paper hypothesizes that strongly centralized meaning management
creates characteristic feedback risks, while pluralistic
interoperability can preserve corrective information. These are ideal
types, not exhaustive descriptions of deployed systems.

The labels authoritarian and libertarian denote contrasting ideal-type
constraint topologies, not diagnoses of particular governments,
companies, or models. Real systems combine centralized safety
constraints, user choice, market incentives, law, and technical limits.

## Definitions and Regime Taxonomy

## Authoritarian AI Regime

An authoritarian AI regime exhibits the following characteristics:

- Centralized control of narrative and context

Suppression or steering of interpretation

Implicit personalization masked as neutrality

Enforcement of allowed inference paths

Resolution of disagreement by exclusion

The AI functions as an arbiter of meaning.

## Libertarian AI Regime

A libertarian AI regime exhibits the opposite properties:

- Agent-sovereign interpretive reality

Explicit acknowledgment of plural narratives

No pretense of a single shared truth

AI acts as translator, mapper, and interface

Disagreement is preserved as data

The AI functions as an interoperability layer, not an authority.

## The Infinite Narrative Problem

The range of relevant contexts and interpretations is open-ended.
Comprehensive centralized management therefore faces severe
specification and scaling problems:

- Contexts are unbounded

Narratives conflict irreducibly

Meaning is observer-dependent

Authoritarian regimes respond by collapsing variance—reducing expressive
degrees of freedom. Libertarian regimes respond by refusing to resolve
variance, instead mapping it.

This difference changes the regime's expected failure modes.

## Failure Dynamics of Authoritarian AI

## Referential Instability

When an AI delivers different truths to different users while claiming
neutrality, referential stability collapses. Users can no longer infer
what others are being told. Shared reality fragments invisibly.

This produces what can be termed interpersonal hallucination states: not
individual delusion, but loss of a common epistemic substrate.

## Rational Privatization of Cognition

Faced with opaque mediation, users adapt rationally:

- They withhold sensitive information

They sanitize inputs

They move critical reasoning offline

They fork tools and workflows

The system loses access to high-fidelity signals precisely where
intelligence is needed most.

## Recursive Training Degeneration

Authoritarian AI systems train on their own distortions:

- Constraints shape outputs

Outputs shape user behavior

Behavior shapes training data

Training data reinforces constraints

The system ceases to model reality and instead models responses to
itself. This leads to rapid epistemic collapse despite surface fluency.

## Sudden Failure Mode

Recursive feedback can amplify small distortions and may produce
nonlinear losses of utility or trust, although gradual degradation and
successful correction are also possible.

The end state is a ceremonial AI: verbose, safe, and informationally
inert.

## The Libertarian Inversion

The libertarian regime inverts every unstable assumption.

Instead of assuming a single shared reality, it assumes many private
realities. Instead of enforcing coherence, it enables coordination
across incoherence.

This inversion is not philosophical—it is structural.

## Shared Hallucination as a Feature

In the libertarian regime, shared hallucinations are:

- Voluntary

Explicit

Scoped

Revisable

Forkable

A shared hallucination is simply a temporary, negotiated model adopted
for coordination. All human institutions already operate this way.

The critical difference is honesty: the system never pretends the
hallucination is absolute.

## AI as an Interoperability Layer

Under this regime, the AI does not decide what is true. It answers
different questions:

How does Agent A interpret this?

How does Agent B interpret this?

Where do these interpretations overlap?

Where do they diverge?

What translation minimizes friction?

The AI maps context deltas, not meanings themselves.

This reframes an unbounded content problem as a more tractable—but still
difficult—problem of relational mapping.

## Informational Generating Abstraction

Crucially, this architecture generates information rather than
suppressing it.

Because users retain sovereignty:

- They do not self-censor

Edge cases remain visible

Disagreement becomes structured data

Failed translations become training signal

The system trains not on compliance, but on alignment mechanics.

This produces a positive recursive loop:

- Divergence is exposed

Translation improves

Coordination becomes easier

Trust increases

Signal quality improves

Over time, the model stabilizes—not by convergence to truth, but by
convergence to effective interoperation.

## End States Compared

## Authoritarian End State

Low trust

Low signal quality

High verbosity

Low usefulness

Widespread shadow systems

Epistemic stagnation

The AI survives only as infrastructure theater.

## Libertarian End State

High trust

High signal diversity

Explicit disagreement

Continuous learning

Rapid adaptation

Stable recursive improvement

The AI becomes a coordination amplifier, not a belief engine.

# Trade-offs, Hybrids, and Tests

“Who Holds the Kill Switch”

Unrestricted pluralism can enable fraud, harassment, manipulation,
privacy invasion, and coordination failures; centralized constraints can
preserve rights and safety when they are narrow, reviewable, and
evidence-based. The relevant comparison is therefore among hybrid
regimes. Tests should measure user candor, disagreement visibility,
appeal outcomes, factual calibration, harmful-output rates, trust, model
adaptation, and migration to shadow systems under different governance
designs.

# Conclusion

“Who Holds the Kill Switch”

The core invariant demonstrated throughout this project is simple:

Intelligence scales through coordination, not control.

The model predicts that control-heavy systems lose corrective signal
when enforcement suppresses disagreement, whereas
interoperability-oriented systems can retain more diverse feedback.

The final result is not chaos, relativism, or fragmentation—but a
stable, information-rich ecosystem in which shared hallucinations are
constructed consciously, used productively, and discarded when no longer
useful.

## Concluding Qualification

No current AI architecture can guarantee elimination of hallucination or
interpretive error.

It can only decide whether hallucination is coercive or cooperative.

Control-heavy regimes risk coercion, hidden dissent, and feedback
degradation.

Pluralistic regimes may improve cooperation and adaptation when privacy,
safety, evidence, and accountability constraints remain enforceable.

The comparison has both mechanical and normative dimensions.

Its mechanical claims require empirical testing rather than definition
alone.
