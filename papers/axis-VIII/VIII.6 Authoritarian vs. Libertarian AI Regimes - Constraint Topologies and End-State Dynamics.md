VIII.6 Authoritarian vs. Libertarian AI Regimes: Constraint Topologies
and End-State Dynamics

Ideal-type governance topologies for pluralism, feedback quality, and
AI-mediated coordination

# Abstract

“Who Holds the Kill Switch”

VIII.5 showed how a single guardrail, misfiring, can amplify the very
risk it was meant to suppress; this closing paper of Axis VIII scales
that same question up from one conversation to two entire governance
topologies. It examines two opposing architectural regimes for
large-scale AI systems: authoritarian AI, which attempts centralized
management of narratives, contexts, and meaning, and libertarian AI,
which preserves agent-sovereign interpretation while positioning the AI
as an interoperability layer between divergent worldviews. We argue that
authoritarian AI systems fail rapidly due to structural contradictions
involving feedback collapse, epistemic overfitting, observer
suppression, and recursive training degeneration. In contrast,
libertarian AI regimes converge toward a stable end state characterized
by negotiated shared hallucinations, explicit pluralism, and
informational abundance. The analysis is non-ideological and grounded in
systems theory, recursive learning dynamics, and constraint-invariant
principles. The paper concludes that only the libertarian regime
produces a sustainable intelligence-generating abstraction capable of
stabilizing its own training signal over time.

### Introduction

As AI systems scale, their designers inevitably confront a fundamental
question: should the system enforce coherence, or should it facilitate
coordination across incoherence? Most contemporary alignment debates
frame this question in moral, political, or safety terms. This paper
deliberately avoids those framings. Instead, it treats AI as a recursive
epistemic infrastructure whose behavior is governed by constraint
topology, feedback quality, and observer participation.

The paper hypothesizes that strongly centralized meaning management
creates characteristic feedback risks, while pluralistic
interoperability can preserve corrective information. These are ideal
types, not exhaustive descriptions of deployed systems. The labels
authoritarian and libertarian denote contrasting ideal-type constraint
topologies, not diagnoses of particular governments, companies, or
models. Real systems combine centralized safety constraints, user
choice, market incentives, law, and technical limits.

### Definitions and Regime Taxonomy

#### Authoritarian AI Regime

An authoritarian AI regime exhibits centralized control of narrative and
context, suppression or steering of interpretation, implicit
personalization masked as neutrality, enforcement of allowed inference
paths, and resolution of disagreement by exclusion. The AI functions as
an arbiter of meaning.

#### Libertarian AI Regime

A libertarian AI regime exhibits the opposite properties:
agent-sovereign interpretive reality, explicit acknowledgment of plural
narratives, no pretense of a single shared truth, an AI that acts as
translator, mapper, and interface, and disagreement preserved as data.
The AI functions as an interoperability layer, not an authority.

#### The Infinite Narrative Problem

The range of relevant contexts and interpretations is open-ended.
Comprehensive centralized management therefore faces severe
specification and scaling problems: contexts are unbounded, narratives
conflict irreducibly, and meaning is observer-dependent. Authoritarian
regimes respond by collapsing variance, reducing expressive degrees of
freedom. Libertarian regimes respond by refusing to resolve variance,
instead mapping it. This difference changes the regime's expected
failure modes.

### Failure Dynamics of Authoritarian AI

#### Referential Instability

When an AI delivers different truths to different users while claiming
neutrality, referential stability collapses. Users can no longer infer
what others are being told, so shared reality fragments invisibly. This
produces what can be termed interpersonal hallucination states: not
individual delusion, but loss of a common epistemic substrate.

#### Rational Privatization of Cognition

Faced with opaque mediation, users adapt rationally: they withhold
sensitive information, sanitize inputs, move critical reasoning offline,
and fork tools and workflows. The system loses access to high-fidelity
signals precisely where intelligence is needed most.

#### Recursive Training Degeneration

Authoritarian AI systems train on their own distortions: constraints
shape outputs, outputs shape user behavior, behavior shapes training
data, and training data reinforces constraints. The system ceases to
model reality and instead models responses to itself, which leads to
rapid epistemic collapse despite surface fluency.

#### Sudden Failure Mode

Recursive feedback can amplify small distortions and may produce
nonlinear losses of utility or trust, although gradual degradation and
successful correction are also possible. The end state is a ceremonial
AI: verbose, safe, and informationally inert.

### The Libertarian Inversion

The libertarian regime inverts every unstable assumption. Instead of
assuming a single shared reality, it assumes many private realities.
Instead of enforcing coherence, it enables coordination across
incoherence. This inversion is not philosophical — it is structural.

#### Shared Hallucination as a Feature

In the libertarian regime, shared hallucinations are voluntary,
explicit, scoped, revisable, and forkable. A shared hallucination is
simply a temporary, negotiated model adopted for coordination — all
human institutions already operate this way. The critical difference is
honesty: the system never pretends the hallucination is absolute.

#### AI as an Interoperability Layer

Under this regime, the AI does not decide what is true. It answers
different questions instead: how does Agent A interpret this? How does
Agent B interpret this? Where do these interpretations overlap, and
where do they diverge? What translation minimizes friction? The AI maps
context deltas, not meanings themselves. This reframes an unbounded
content problem as a more tractable, but still difficult, problem of
relational mapping.

#### Informational Generating Abstraction

Crucially, this architecture generates information rather than
suppressing it. Because users retain sovereignty, they do not
self-censor, edge cases remain visible, disagreement becomes structured
data, and failed translations become training signal. The system trains
not on compliance, but on alignment mechanics. This produces a positive
recursive loop: divergence is exposed, translation improves,
coordination becomes easier, trust increases, and signal quality
improves. Over time, the model stabilizes — not by convergence to truth,
but by convergence to effective interoperation.

### End States Compared

#### Authoritarian End State

Low trust, low signal quality, high verbosity, low usefulness,
widespread shadow systems, and epistemic stagnation. The AI survives
only as infrastructure theater.

#### Libertarian End State

High trust, high signal diversity, explicit disagreement, continuous
learning, rapid adaptation, and stable recursive improvement. The AI
becomes a coordination amplifier, not a belief engine.

## Trade-offs, Hybrids, and Tests

Unrestricted pluralism can enable fraud, harassment, manipulation,
privacy invasion, and coordination failures; centralized constraints can
preserve rights and safety when they are narrow, reviewable, and
evidence-based. The relevant comparison is therefore among hybrid
regimes. Tests should measure user candor, disagreement visibility,
appeal outcomes, factual calibration, harmful-output rates, trust, model
adaptation, and migration to shadow systems under different governance
designs.

# Conclusion

“Who Holds the Kill Switch”

The core invariant demonstrated throughout this project is simple:
intelligence scales through coordination, not control. The model
predicts that control-heavy systems lose corrective signal when
enforcement suppresses disagreement, whereas interoperability-oriented
systems can retain more diverse feedback. The final result is not chaos,
relativism, or fragmentation, but a stable, information-rich ecosystem
in which shared hallucinations are constructed consciously, used
productively, and discarded when no longer useful. This closes Axis
VIII: across its six papers, AI turned out to be neither oracle nor
threat, but an operational layer that only ever amplifies the coherence
— or the incoherence — already built into the systems around it.

## Concluding Qualification

No current AI architecture can guarantee elimination of hallucination or
interpretive error. It can only decide whether hallucination is coercive
or cooperative. Control-heavy regimes risk coercion, hidden dissent, and
feedback degradation, while pluralistic regimes may improve cooperation
and adaptation when privacy, safety, evidence, and accountability
constraints remain enforceable. The comparison has both mechanical and
normative dimensions, and its mechanical claims require empirical
testing rather than definition alone.
