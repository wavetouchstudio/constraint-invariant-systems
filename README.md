# A Constraint-Invariant Awareness Framework for Meaning-Bearing Systems

Coordinated systems exhibit second-order behavioral regularities that arise from coordination constraints rather than individual intent, ideology, or narrative. Most large-scale harm in human systems emerges non-maliciously, through procedurally correct operation of rules and incentives that exceed the system's capacity for contextual judgment, feedback integration, and agency preservation.

This corpus documents that pattern as it recurs across eleven domains: constraint theory, institutional governance, harm and abuse, shame and social enforcement, narrative and symbolic control, language and cognition, AI alignment, image and collective pathology, coordination economics, and physical/aerospace systems.

## What This Is

- Structural, comparative, and invariant-seeking
- Falsifiable by failure — an invariant is rejected if it fails under differentiation, breaks under frame rotation, or loses explanatory power when compressed
- Executable through use — meant to be queried, tested, and mapped onto a reader's own domain expertise, not memorized or agreed with

## What This Is Not

- Not a first-principles theory or a grand unified theory
- Not prophecy, ideology, or a political program
- Not a knowledge repository or encyclopedia — it discards content deliberately to extract structural regularities
- Not a replacement for domain expertise in law, medicine, engineering, economics, or governance
- Not complete, and not intended to be

Read [`meta/What This Isn't.html`](meta/What%20This%20Isn%E2%80%99t.html) for the full boundary statement.

## How to Read This

Read for structure, not conclusion. Read for invariants, not opinions. You are not expected to agree with every example or framing — you are expected to notice what keeps reappearing across domains you already understand.

A correct interaction looks like: *"Does this invariant appear here, and under what conditions does it fail?"*
An incorrect interaction looks like: *"Do I agree with this worldview?"*

Start with, in order:

1. [Preface](meta/Preface.html)
2. [Functional Definition](meta/Functional%20Definition.html)
3. [Interpretive Integrity and Scope Clarification](meta/Interpretive%20Integrity%20and%20Scope%20Clarification.html) — load-bearing; prevents the most common misreadings (moral inference, conspiratorial causation, literalized symbolism) before they happen

## Structure

- **79 canonical papers** across **11 axes** (`papers/axis-I/` through `papers/axis-XI/`)
- **[Concept graph](graphify-out/graph.html)** — extracted concepts and their relationships across the full corpus
- **[Topic index](topics.html)** — 10 cross-cutting themes that span multiple axes (e.g. Shame & Social Enforcement, Governance Failure, AI Alignment)
- **Related Papers** — inline cross-links between papers that cite or build on one another
- Many papers carry a quoted colloquial alt-title beneath the formal title (e.g. *"How Records Ruin Lives Slowly"*). The quotation marks are load-bearing: these are pre-theoretical descriptions of a phenomenon people already name informally, not claims the author endorses. See [Why This Might Sound Strange at First](meta/%E2%80%9CWhy%20This%20Might%20Sound%20Strange%20at%20First%E2%80%9D.html).
